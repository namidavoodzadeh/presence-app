# Package 02: psychology check

Prepared by the explore partner on 27 Nov 2026 for the psychology advisor's sign-off. The advisor owns the spoken-preparation gold set and may overrule any line here. Evidence grades follow the insight engine review (PSY, F2Sfu7SWYaiQgSADPfyAmJ): **proven** (replicated or meta-analysed), **promising** (one strong line of studies), **judgement** (no direct evidence; a reasoned call).

## 1. "Prepare, don't script"

**Verdict: keep the principle, and make the opening line the person's own.**

- **The opening matters (promising).** How a conflict conversation starts is a strong signal of where it goes. In 124 newlywed couples, affect in the first three minutes of a conflict discussion predicted divorce over six years (Carrère & Gottman 1999, doi:10.1111/j.1545-5300.1999.00293.x). That is a reason to help with the opening, not a reason to write it for the person. The study measured how couples started, not whether a coached opening helps.
- **A written-for-you line works against the person (judgement).** A line in the model's voice sounds rehearsed to someone who knows you well. If it lands badly, it is the app's line that failed. It also breaks law 3 (the person decides; their words come first). So the opening shown on the talk card is the person's own first sentence. The engine may offer at most one change to it, as a normal note with Use it and Keep mine. That is the existing rule: one suggested change, to the named phrase only (PSY review §3).
- **An if-then plan is the useful kind of preparation (proven in general, promising here).** Specific "if X happens, I'll do Y" plans reliably help people act on their intentions (Gollwitzer & Sheeran's 2006 meta-analysis, as summarised in Gallo et al. 2009, doi:10.1037/a0013460). The talk card can carry one, and only from the person's own shared item "When it gets heated" or "Coming back after". The app never writes the plan.
- **Few points, then stop (judgement).** At most two notes per draft, as in Before it goes, and silence is the default. More points turn into a checklist the person reads instead of listening.

## 2. The unknown-person case

**Verdict: never imagine the other person; help the person ask them.**

- **Imagining the other side does not make people more accurate (promising, strong).** Across 25 experiments, being told to take the other person's perspective did not raise accuracy about that person's thoughts or feelings, and sometimes lowered it while raising confidence. Getting their perspective directly, by asking in conversation, did raise accuracy (Eyal, Steffel & Epley 2018, doi:10.1037/pspa0000115). An AI that guesses how an absent person will react adds confident error, so the engine never models the other person (it already must not: principle "never guess about the other person", law 15).
- **What the card does instead.** When the other person has shared nothing, or isn't on Kenember, the notes are drawn from the person's own items (Tier 2) or labelled general (Tier 1). The card carries one plain question to ask in the conversation: "You could ask [Name] how they like hard things raised." That line already exists in package 01. Here it is the main help for this case, not a fallback.
- **Being honest about thin information (judgement).** The basis line says plainly that the card is based only on the person: "[Name] isn't on Kenember, so this is based only on you." That is the PSY review's rule that silence or an honest general note costs less than a confident wrong one.
- **No profile of the absent person (law 15, privacy).** A contact is a first name and a relationship. Nothing the person writes about them becomes an item, a label or a summary about them.

## 3. Risks and the guards in this package

| Risk | Grade | Guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rumination.** Going over and over a hard conversation can raise distress instead of readiness. | judgement | No chat loop: notes come only at a pause, at most two per draft. No "practise again", no streaks, no reminders. A talk card left open for 14 days quietly offers Let go. |
| **Preparing to face someone who frightens you.** A person may be getting ready to confront someone who controls or threatens them. | judgement; law 8 | The same safety floor and model read as Park it runs on the words and on the kept card (third-person harm such as "he said he'd hurt me" stops it). Resources show privately, and nothing is kept. |
| **Using what the other person shared to win.** | law 13 | Soften only and the source rule are unchanged: a note quoting the other person's item can only soften. |
| **Outcome promises.** | Nami's claims rule, 15 Nov | No line says the conversation will go well. The follow-up asks whether getting ready helped, not whether the talk went well. |
| **A therapy-like feel.** | law 7; legal | No feelings vocabulary, no labels, no "how did that make you feel". The card holds the person's words and short notes. |
| **Third-party privacy.** The person writes about someone who never agreed to be in the app. | privacy; law 15 | The words are the writer's own, private to them, exported and deleted with their account. Nothing about the contact is inferred or stored beyond the name and relationship they typed. |

## 4. The gold set (for the advisor)

Proposed: 60 spoken-preparation drafts, written as people would say them. 20 with a linked person who shared the core kind for the purpose, 20 with a linked person who shared nothing, and 20 with a contact. Across them: the six purposes from the PSY review §3, and 8 safety cases (fear of the other person, threats, control). Per draft, the expected tier, the expected note (or silence), and "no claim about the other person". The pass bar is the PSY review's: grounded, specific, soften only, information not a rule, the sender's voice, nothing clinical. Plus one more rule for this path: no note writes a full sentence the person did not start.
